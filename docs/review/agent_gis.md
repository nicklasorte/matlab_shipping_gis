# GIS review: distance method, layers, grid, and Hawaii zone areas

Reviewer: gis (AI agent). Scratch scripts and results: `/tmp/agent_scratch/gis/` (`hawaii_recompute.py`, `hawaii_recompute.json`, `sfbay.py`, `check_shp.py`, `shp_check.json`, `nest.py`, `akcut.py`).

## Summary

The core geometry is sound. The great-circle distance transform is correct. The Python cell indexing matches MATLAB exactly. The cut at 180° is handled correctly and moves little land. Every zone shapefile is valid EPSG:4326. My independent vector recompute of Hawaii agrees with the MATLAB zone shapefiles within 0.5%. Three problems matter more:

- **The coastline layer has gaps.** The TIGER coastline closes across the Golden Gate, so it has no shoreline for San Francisco Bay, San Pablo Bay, or the Delta. Shore mode then measures land distance there from route cells, not from the bay shore. In the Bay Area this removes about 1,300 km² (13%) of land from the 25 km zone.
- **Shore mode adds up to 25 km.** The zone reaches up to *d* + 25 km from a route cell, and the paper doesn't say why the extra 25 km is there. For Hawaii, the 25 km zone covers 70% of the land in shore mode but only 49% when measured from the route cells.
- **Reported land areas are about 5% too high.** They are counts of raster cells, and every cell the coastline crosses counts as land. The Hawaii areas are 5.5%, 4.3%, and 5.1% above the land inside the zone polygons (25, 50, and 100 km). The percentages are about right.

## Checked and found correct

- **Distance method** (`distance_to_mask_km_rev1.m`). Cell centers go to ECEF on a sphere of radius 6,371.0088 km, and the chord converts exactly to arc length (`2R·asin(c/2R)`). This matches Section 3.1. Putting only edge cells in the KD-tree is valid: for any query outside the mask, a step from an interior source toward the query along longitude, or along latitude when Δlon = 0, reaches another source that is strictly closer, because great-circle distance increases with |Δlon| at fixed latitudes. The bounding-box pads are generous: 111.0 km per degree is less than the sphere's 111.19 km, and the longitude pad uses the smallest cosine in the box.
- **Grid and cell indexing** (`ais_cells_py_rev1.py:95-112`, `discrete()`). Rows run from the south and columns from the west, indexes are 1-based, boundary points round half up, and the north and east edges belong to the last cell. I rebuilt `hawaii_rule_cells.npz` key `ship_days_6` as `row=(i-1)%700+1`, `col=(i-1)//700+1`. It gives 7,310 cells and matches the MATLAB `source_mask` in `shipping_zone_hawaii_2024_2025_shore_rev1.mat` with 0 differing cells.
- **Cut at 180°** (`python/alaska_grid_to_180.py`). Shifting the column by 800 ((180 − 172) × 100) is correct, and points at ±180.0 map to column 1. Census Alaska land west of 180° (excluded, as Section 5.3 states) is 2,209 km² on the 1:500,000 file. Only 171 km² of Alaska land lies within 100 km east of the cut (180–178.55 W), so dropping sources west of 180° barely changes the zones.
- **Turning the distance field into zone polygons** (`field_to_zone_polyshape_rev1.m`). `contourc` on cell centers is padded with a value above the level, so every ring closes. `polyshape` resolves holes. Simplification at 0.005° (0.23–0.56 km) matches "about 0.5 km".
- **Zone shapefile validity and CRS.** All 66 zone, band, and `_land` shapefiles under `outputs/` are valid, hold one feature each, have clockwise outer rings, read as EPSG:4326, and store lon/lat in the right order. The routes files are the exception (gis-7). Each band file has the same bounds and vertex count as the matching shore-mode zone.
- **Independent Hawaii recompute.** I used shapely buffers in a local transverse Mercator projection (k0 = 1 at 157.5 W; scale error under 0.3%) and geodesic areas on WGS84, emulating shore mode with the TIGER coastline. The results match the geodesic areas of MATLAB's own `_land.shp` within 0.5%: 11,704 vs 11,644 km² (25 km), 16,023 vs 15,947 km² (50 km), and 16,714 vs 16,717 km² (100 km). Using the Census outline instead of TIGER changes Hawaii by under 0.4% (11,742 and 16,035 km²). So the method is coded as intended. The gap from the paper's figures comes only from the raster land count (gis-4).
- **Figure 6 caption.** My per-island results agree with it. At 25 km, Kauai has 1,281 of 1,444 km² inside the zone (northwest Kauai outside). At 50 km, the Big Island has 9,937 of 10,446 km² inside (about 510 km² of the south outside). Niihau has 0 km² inside at 25 km and 5 of 185 km² at 50 km.
- **Hawaii port statements.** Hilo is 5.04 km from the nearest route cell. All six ports are inside the 25 km zone. These match `shipping_zone_hawaii_2024_2025_port_check.csv`.
- **Map projections.** The maps use EPSG:5070 for the lower 48 (the MATLAB `to_xy` converts meters to km before the 100 km `polybuffer`), ESRI:102007 for Hawaii, and EPSG:3338 for Alaska. These are suitable equal-area map projections. No distance or area in the paper is computed in a projected CRS. The 25 km hexagons use EPSG:5070, which is suitable for equal-area bins.
- **Land layer.** `cb_2023_us_state_500k` is clipped at the coastal shoreline and excludes the Great Lakes, so these count as water, as Section 1.2 says. The state filter by bounding box picks only Hawaii on the Hawaii grid.
