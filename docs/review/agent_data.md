# Data review: where the large-ship routes are wrong

Reviewer: data (AI agent). Scratch work: `/tmp/agent_scratch/data/` (QC copies and outputs in `qc/`, analysis scripts in `work/`).

## Summary

I reran the data-validity checks on two new days, 2024-10-09 and 2025-04-09. Both days fall inside the ranges in `outputs/2024_2025/qc/qc_summary.md`, so the raw data on those days is no worse than on the days already checked. The routes still have errors, though, and most of them come from the filter, not from bad AIS reports. The worst one is on the Mississippi. The 35 km of route above Baton Rouge, to 30.77° N, comes from the two-ship port-approach rule, and two 100 m U.S. river cruise vessels supply it. Seagoing S-band ships don't go there. The 100 m test also keeps a cluster of ships that report exactly 100.0 m: state ferries, Long Island Sound ferries, offshore supply vessels, and river cruise vessels. The tug-barge rule adds 638 route cells along the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, inland of the coast. About 40% of the Pacific route cells are off Mexico. That raises the Pacific offshore-distance statistic from 83 km to 132 km. It also shows that the 2024–2025 files contain minute-by-minute reports 300–500 km off Baja California, which shore receivers can't pick up. The NOAA transit-count cross-check reads an 800 m overview of the raster that drops narrow channels. At 200 m, 97.9% of route cells have NOAA traffic, and nearly every route cell without it is off Baja. Hilo is off the network because of a reception gap, which I confirmed in the raw data. Away from these cases, I found no route cells on land away from water.

## Checked and found correct

- **Data validity on two new days.** I reran `ais_qc_day.py` (scratch copy, output redirected) on 2024-10-09 and 2025-04-09. Lower 48: bad MMSI 0.0% on both days. Bad IMO 0.2% on both. Jumps 10.8% and 10.7%. Shared MMSI 7.4% and 8.0%. Reports more than 2 km inland and off the routes: 0.67% and 0.62% (18 and 16 ships). Route cells made only by flagged reports: 833 (0.126%) and 730 (0.111%). Hawaii and Puerto Rico and the USVI: 0% on every check except one Hawaii ship with a jump on 2024-10-09. All of these fall inside the ranges in `qc_summary.md`. No report falls outside its day.
- **What the flags are.** Most jumps and shared MMSIs are two ships transmitting one MMSI. Each ship's track is plausible on its own (for example, MMSI 218834000 alternates between two tracks about 160 km apart off central California). The flag-only route cells cluster off central California. These pairs lower the distinct-ship counts; they don't add false cells.
- **Inland reports off the routes on the new days.** These are ships moored in rivers and canals that the 1:500,000 shoreline closes (Coos Bay, Port Jefferson, Houma, Charleston, the Columbia), plus the river cruise vessels in data-1 and data-2. None is a position error far from water.
- **Route cells on land.** Apart from the cells in data-3, every route cell more than 5 km inside Census land lies on a known ship channel: the lower Mississippi, the Houston and Sabine-Neches channels, the Calcasieu, the Columbia, the Hudson to Albany, the Delaware, the Sacramento and San Joaquin, the James to Hopewell, the Cooper, the Savannah, the St. Johns, and the Cape Fear.
- **Port checks.** In the lower-48 files: 69 of 73 deep-draft ports are on the network. 71 of 73 are within 5 km and all 73 are inside the 25 km zone. The four that aren't on the network are Portland, Oregon (5.1 km); Illinois International Port (12.2 km); Panama City; and Searsport. All 24 named ports are within 1.71 km. Hawaii: Hilo 5.0 km, Kawaihae 24.8 km. Puerto Rico and the USVI: 7 of 8 ports within 0.77 km, Mayaguez 29.7 km. Alaska: Anchorage 0.3 km, Valdez 0.6 km, Nikiski and Dutch Harbor 0.3 km, Kivalina 11.9 km. Each value matches the text.
- **Kapsar cross-check and the satellite cutoff.** The values in `kapsar_check.json` support the text: 94.8% of the busy satellite hexagons without a shore-receiver route are beyond 74 km. A search over every distinct cutoff value finds the best near-shore Jaccard index, 0.581, at 307 operating days (373 hexagons), against 0.571 at the paper's 272. The difference is small and falls inside the ±2× sensitivity range in Section 5.3. The 318,134 added cells match the run log (334,651 satellite cells in all, 318,134 not already route cells).
- **Busy waters with no route.** At 200 m, the NOAA top-1% cells more than 10 km from a route cluster in Buzzards and Narragansett bays, Barataria and Fourchon, Pamlico Sound, the Atchafalaya, Red, and Ouachita rivers, Penobscot Bay, Florida Bay, and the Potomac and York rivers. That's ferry, fishing, offshore-supply, and towboat water. The only large-ship gaps are the Searsport approach through Penobscot Bay, which the paper already reports, and the Navy sites in data-14.
- **Route-cell totals.** The total in Section A.1 (658,867) and the river end at 30.485° N in A.4 match `viz_cells.npz` and `viz_summary.json`.
