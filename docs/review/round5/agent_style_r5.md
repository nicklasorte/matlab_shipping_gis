## Long replacements

**Long fix A (R5STY-1), Sec. 1.5 item 4, replacing from "Charted lanes are other countries'" to the end of the item:**

> Charted lanes include other countries' routing measures as well as U.S. ones, and they show charted routes, not observed traffic. The analysis therefore keeps only charted-lane cells nearer to U.S. land than to foreign land, as an equidistance stand-in for the maritime boundary. This drops 3,427 lower-48 cells and, in Alaska, 6,800 cells near Russia and Canada. Observed ship-track cells are kept wherever they are. Near Russia, the 1990 boundary line isn't an equidistance line; an earlier check found that the land in the Alaska zones changed by about 11 km².

**Long fix B (R5STY-2), Sec. 5.1 "Satellite lanes" bullet:**

> **Satellite lanes.** By Coast Guard figures, shore receivers reach about 24 nautical miles for two-way use and up to 50 nautical miles for receiving; in Alaska, the Marine Exchange of Alaska owns most receivers. This analysis assumes a reach of about 40 nautical miles (74 km), so the ship tracks miss busy lanes farther out. The calibration hasn't been tested at 50 nautical miles (93 km). The satellite-based North Pacific and Arctic Marine Vessel Traffic Dataset ([Kapsar and others, 2022](https://doi.org/10.18739/A2XG9FC41), CC BY 4.0) fills those lanes. It counts cargo and tanker operating days in 625 km² hexagons. This analysis summed the operating days over 2018–2019, kept the hexagons at or above a cutoff, and rasterized them to the 0.01° grid, clipped to water. The cutoff (272 operating days over 2018–2019) is the best of a coarse search for the cutoff whose busy hexagons best match the shore-receiver routes within 74 km of shore, where those routes are complete (Jaccard index 0.57). Of the 400 hexagons at or above the cutoff, the base keeps only the 290 beyond shore-receiver range; the other 110 lie within 74 km of shore, where ship tracks are taken as complete. The 290 add 246,400 water cells, 245,735 of them not already route cells. Because they lie offshore, they add little land: without them, the land in the 25, 50, and 100 km zones would be 90,923, 181,070, and 317,670 km², against 90,926, 181,074, and 319,271 km² with them.

## Patterns

These patterns run through the paper. Rows above fix the worst cases; the rest are for a later style pass.

- **Very long sentences and list items.** The worst are Sec. 1.5 item 4, Sec. 5.1 "Satellite lanes", Sec. 5.3 "Satellite cutoff", the Sec. 3 paragraph on the foreign-water clip (about 280 words), and Sec. 1.4 "How well length stands in for gross tonnage". A safe target is under 30 words per sentence and under 120 words per list item.
- **"Against" as the comparison word (46 times).** It's clear once a reader knows it, but it's unusual in U.S. technical prose. Prefer "compared with" in body text, and keep "against" for plotted axes ("tonnage against length").
- **Passive voice in the method.** For example: "are added", "is checked against", "were matched", "are kept", "is removed". Many are fine, because the actor is "the analysis". Where the actor matters, name it: "The code checks the download against NOAA's feature count."
- **Hedge stacking.** For example, "likely because reports thin out ..., perhaps from a receiver gap" (Sec. 4.2). Use one hedge per claim.
- **Draft history in the body.** "Earlier draft" appears three times (R5STY-21). Keep the paper in the present tense about the current method.
- **Color-only legends in captions** (R5STY-16).
- **ISO dates in table headers.** Tables 1 and 2 use "2024-07-10", while the text uses "July 10, 2024". Google style prefers "July 10, 2024", or "Jul 10, 2024" where space is tight.
- **Inconsistent port names in Table 5.** Corps names such as "South Louisiana, LA, Port of" and "Coos Bay OR, Port of" don't match Table 4 ("South Louisiana, LA"). Either note in the Table 5 note that names are as published by the Corps, or normalize them.
- **Imperial units in Appendix B.** Table B-2 gives antenna length in feet only. Table B-1 pairs feet with meters; do the same in Table B-2, or note that lengths are as stated by the manufacturer.
- **British spelling in alt text.** Figure A-18 alt text uses "labelled". Use "labeled".
- **Word copy metadata.** `core_properties.title` drops the colon ("2.7 GHz study Large-ship routes ...") and the document language is blank. Set the title to the H1 text and the language to en-US so the accessibility checker passes.
- **Forward references to tables.** Table 9 is first cited in the Sec. 1.2 data table, and Table 8 in Sec. 1.5, before Tables 3 to 7. Table 5 is first cited in Sec. 1.6, before Table 4 (Sec. 1.8). Numbering by order of appearance is correct and the forward references are acceptable; no change is needed beyond R5STY-25.
