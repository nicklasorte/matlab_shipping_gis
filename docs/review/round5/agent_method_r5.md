# Round 5 method and code review

Reviewer: blind method and code agent. Paper snapshot: `docs/review/round5/paper_rev616.md` (doc revision 616). Scope: Sections 1, 3, 4, and 5, the decision-table runs (Table 8), population and Partial Economic Area (PEA) counts, the Alaska satellite lanes, tests, and failure paths. The reviewer read the MATLAB code (MATLAB wasn't available), ran the Python tests, and reran the population and PEA scripts from the committed zone files. The reviewer didn't read `docs/review/` (other than the paper) or `docs/plans/`.

## Summary

The core zone code does what Sections 1 and 3 say. The ship filter, the per-year distinct-ship threshold, the ship-days rule, the port-approach rule with its 25 km coastline limit, the tug-barge coastline band, the 5 km distance margin for the 100 km boundary, the equidistance water clip, and the 290-hexagon Alaska selection all match the text. The Python tests pass (13 passed; the known-answer test is exact), and the committed zone files reproduce every population count in Table 8 and the PEA summary exactly. The main problems are in what two method choices actually test and in what the PEA count leaves out. First, the Great Lakes layer is a geographic box, not a set of ships: it removes the routes of ocean-going SOLAS ships on the Lakes along with the lakers that SOLAS leaves out, and its eastern edge (74.5° W) doesn't match the SOLAS limit at Montreal. Second, the PEA count uses only the 412 PEAs that have Census block groups; the U.S. Virgin Islands PEA and the Gulf of Mexico PEA, both inside the zones, drop out of the count and the denominator. Third, the run logs can't be tied to a code version: the base run, the variant runs, and the committed `shipping_zone_rev1.m` are three different versions by line number. Other gaps a challenger could use: no route cells on the St. Lawrence below Montreal (an unstated coverage edge that would enlarge the northern New England zones), a stale Alaska cell count in Section 5.3, a tug-barge rule whose effect rests on a 2024 change in how AIS reports tow length, missing Python twins for the zone polygons and land areas, untested decision-variant wiring, and several silent skips in the population and decision-table scripts.

## What was checked and found sound

- **Ship filter.** `ais_large_ship_cells_rev1.m` and `ais_cells_py_rev1.py` apply the Section 1.4 types (0, 33, 35, 55, 60-99; towing 31, 32, 52 separately), 100 m length, IMO-field test with the 35 and 55 exception, and the grid test. A blank length or type fails, and unreadable MMSIs drop in both twins. The length prefilter is never stricter than the filter.
- **Ship threshold.** `route_year_counts_rev1.m` counts distinct MMSIs per calendar year and takes the larger year (`max_year_ships`); the both-years run uses the smaller. Known-answer tests cover both, and a year with no data.
- **Ship-days rule (Hawaii, Alaska).** Distinct ships per day, summed over the 104 days, at least 6; port-approach rule off; tug-barge pairs merged first, as in the Python twin. Logs give 7,310 Hawaii cells and 26,983 Alaska ship-track cells, matching the paper.
- **Port-approach rule.** 73 coastal and Great Lakes ports with 1 million tons or more of foreign trade; 50 km from the port point; 2 ships in one year; limited to 25 km of the TIGER coastline. Matches Section 1.5 and the logs (11,682 cells; 1,564 in Puerto Rico).
- **Tug-barge band.** Towing pairs kept only in cells within 25 km of TIGER coastline cells, before counting.
- **100 km boundary fix.** The distance field runs to `max_km + 5` km (`shipping_zone_rev1.m:242-245`); the base 100 km land rose by 3,450 km² against the November 14 run, as expected for a half-cell shift.
- **Water clip.** `foreign_side_polygon_rev1.m` marks water nearer foreign land (outline vertices, including past the grid edge) than U.S. land, keeps water beyond 370.4 km of both, and never cuts U.S. land; sources in foreign water stay sources. Matches Section 3.
- **Great Lakes partition.** The two runs split the final source mask (after lanes, racons, and satellite cells) into exact complements by cell center, so their zones' union equals the base. Land adds to more than the base (301,652 against 301,168 km² at 25 km), as the paper says.
- **Decision variants.** Each run changes one setting, writes its own outputs, and errors on an unknown name. Land figures in Table 8 match the logs.
- **Alaska satellite selection.** The cutoff (272 days) is the best near-shore Jaccard match (0.571) among quantile candidates; the sweep shows 300 marginally better (0.573), as Section 5.3 says. 400 hexagons pass, 290 lie beyond 74 km (centroid), adding 246,400 cells. `read_extra_cells_rev1.m` errors on indices outside the grid.
- **Population.** Region filters (lower-48 box without AK, HI, PR; HI; PR; AK) give denominators equal to the 2020 totals (329,260,619; 1,455,271; 3,285,874; 733,391). The committed zone files reproduce every base and variant population in `outputs/zone_population_rev1.csv`. The county remaps (Valdez-Cordova split, Kusilvak, Oglala Lakota) run in the right direction and leave no block group unmatched. No Alaska block-group center lies west of 180°.
- **Tests.** `python3 -m pytest -q python/`: 13 passed. `python3 python/test_known_answer.py`: exact for both grids, and the position-jump flag fires.
