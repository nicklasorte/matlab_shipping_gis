# Federal operations review: places where S-band ships may run without AIS

Reviewer: fedops (AI agent). Public sources only. Draft review of `docs/review/paper_under_review.md` (Nov 10, 2026) and the zone shapefiles.

## Summary

I checked 127 public federal places against the paper's 25, 50, and 100 km band zones. The places are Navy homeports and shipyards, Coast Guard cutter homeports, NOAA ship homeports, sample points in Navy at-sea range complexes, and named 33 CFR Part 334 areas. I also checked all 422 polygons in NOAA's public Danger Zones and Restricted Areas layer. The news is mostly good. Every Navy, Coast Guard, and NOAA homeport point lies inside the 100 km zone, and all but one lie inside the 25 km zone. Every Part 334 area in Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Alaska is fully inside all three zones. No federal place that ships likely to carry S-band radar use lies outside every zone. The only areas outside the 100 km zone are two inland Army river areas in Tennessee and Kentucky. The gaps are partial, and they cluster in two patterns. First, three places that matter lie outside the 25 km zone: the Pacific Missile Range Facility (PMRF) on Kauai, NOAA's Pacific ship base at Newport, Oregon, and the Navy test ranges on the Potomac River. Second, at several shipyards and submarine bases at the head of an estuary, the base sits inside the zone, but the land around it doesn't. A ship there would reach farther inland than the zone. This happens at Bath Iron Works, Groton, Bangor and Dabob Bay, Kings Bay, Mare Island, and the upper Cooper River. One code rule causes most of the coastal gap. Where the shipping lane runs more than 25 km offshore, the code measures land distance from the lane, not from the edge of ship water. This leaves the Olympic coast and much of the Oregon and northern California coast outside the 25 km zone. Section 1.10 says that Navy ships turn AIS off, but it doesn't say where that matters. It doesn't mention Coast Guard or NOAA ships. It also doesn't say that the 100 m filter drops Coast Guard medium endurance cutters even when their AIS is on.

**Method.** Point coordinates are approximate (within about 1 to 2 km) and come from public base locations. Points in Navy range complexes are representative inshore points, not boundaries. For each place and each distance D, "uncovered land" is the U.S. land (Census `cb_2023_us_state_500k`) within D of the place, or of the Part 334 polygon, that falls outside the D zone. I measured it on a local azimuthal equidistant projection on a 6,371 km sphere. A ship that transmits from that place reaches that land, so a zone drawn for that ship should cover it. Distance to route is the distance to the nearest vertex of the region's `_routes.shp`. I used these zone files: `outputs/2024_2025/shipping_zone_2024_2025_band_{2900-3000MHz_25km, 3000-3030MHz_50km, 3030-3070MHz_100km}.shp`, the matching `hawaii/` and `prusvi/` files, and `outputs/2018_2019/alaska/`. I downloaded the Part 334 polygons from the NOAA Office for Coastal Management (OCM) FeatureServer and simplified them by 0.002°. Simplifying collapsed 63 very small pier-side polygons, which I left out of the polygon counts. The point checks still cover those bases. The scripts and full results are in `/tmp/agent_scratch/fedops/` (`check.py`, `results.csv`, `dz.py`, `dz_results.csv`, `coast.py`).

## Checked and found correct

- **Navy homeports and shipyards (37 points).** All are inside the 25, 50, and 100 km zones. They include Norfolk, Norfolk Naval Shipyard, Little Creek, Newport News, Yorktown, Mayport, Kings Bay, Charleston, Earle, Groton, Kittery, Bath, Newport, San Diego, North Island, Point Loma, Seal Beach, Port Hueneme, Everett, Bremerton, Bangor, Indian Island, Pascagoula, Austal Mobile, Marinette, Port Canaveral, Key West, Panama City, Pensacola, Ingleside, Philadelphia, Concord, Mare Island, Manchester, Blount Island, Sunny Point, and Pearl Harbor. Norfolk, San Diego, Mayport, Pearl Harbor, and most Puget Sound bases have zero uncovered land at every distance.
- **Coast Guard cutter homeports (26 points).** All are inside every zone. Uncovered land is zero or small, under 60 km² (largest at New London, home of the training ship Eagle: 47 / 59 / 14 km²). The list covers national security cutters at Alameda, Charleston, and Honolulu, icebreakers at Seattle, medium endurance cutters at Portsmouth, Newport, Cape Canaveral, Key West, Pensacola, Port Angeles, and Kodiak, the Coast Guard Yard, and Great Lakes and Alaska tender ports.
- **NOAA ship homeports (10 points).** All are inside the 50 and 100 km zones. All but Newport, Oregon (fedops-2), are inside the 25 km zone, with zero uncovered land.
- **Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.** All 11 Part 334 polygons (Vieques, Culebra, Vieques Passage, St. Croix, north coast) are fully inside all zones, with zero uncovered land. San Juan Coast Guard is inside.
- **Alaska.** The Southeast Alaska Acoustic Measurement Facility (Behm Canal, 334.1275), Adak (Kuluk Bay), Cape Prince of Wales, Lutak Inlet, and Knik Arm are fully inside all zones. The Gulf of Alaska Temporary Maritime Activities Area (TMAA) inshore point and Blying Sound have no land within reach that the zones miss.
- **Hawaii, Oahu.** Barbers Point, Keahi Point, Kaneohe, Makapuu, and Kahoolawe (334.1340(a)(2)) are fully inside all zones, with zero uncovered land.
- **Range-complex inshore points.** Virginia Capes, Cherry Point, Jacksonville, Southern California (San Clemente and San Nicolas), the Point Mugu Sea Range, and Narragansett Bay–Boston: zero uncovered land at every distance.
- **Code against paper.** The paper's statement that military and law enforcement ships are kept without an IMO number matches the code (`init_shipping_zone_rev1.m:43`; `ais_cells_py_rev1.py:66`). Figure 6's note that northwest Kauai falls outside the 25 km zone matches the shapefile.
