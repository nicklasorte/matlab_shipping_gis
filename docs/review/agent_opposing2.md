# Opposing reader review (opposing2)

Role: a skeptical, technically strong outside party that wants smaller zones, or no zones. Paper: `docs/review/paper_under_review.md`. Code and outputs: `outputs/2024_2025`, `outputs/2018_2019`. I did not download raw AIS files. Scratch scripts are in `/tmp/agent_scratch/opposing2/`.

## Summary

The route and filter work holds up. I re-derived the main numbers (Tables 1 to 6, 8, the length-proxy statistics, and most of the Appendix counts) from the shipped CSV, NPZ, and JSON files, and nearly all of them match. The weak points are in how the routes become zones, not in the AIS counting.

The strongest objection is that the "zone distance" in Table 7 and Figure 4 is not a distance from ships. Land is measured from any coastline within 25 km of a route cell, so the nominal 25 km zone reaches up to about 50 km from the nearest route cell. About one fifth of the 25 km zone lies farther than 25 km from every route source. Next hardest to answer are the missing EMC basis for any distance (Section 2 is empty), the assumption that every position at any speed is a transmitting radar, and routes that rest on cells seen by only two ships (the Mississippi above Baton Rouge). Several smaller items are wrong or unsupported numbers: the stated Baton Rouge extension contradicts Appendix A.4, reported land areas run 2 to 8 percent above the shipped polygons, and the length-proxy check covers only 84% of the ships it is quoted for. An opponent would also use the selective stability statistics and the circular Hawaii calibration.
