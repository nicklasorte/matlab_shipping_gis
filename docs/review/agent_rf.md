# RF engineering review: Sections 1.1 and 3, and RF claims elsewhere

Reviewer: rf (AI agent). Sources: the paper (`docs/review/paper_under_review.md`), the repo code and outputs, and the public sources cited by URL in the table.

## Summary

The core carriage rule in Section 1.1 is stated correctly. SOLAS V/19.2.7.1 requires a 3 GHz radar, or a second 9 GHz radar, on ships of 3,000 GT and up. The 100 m length test is a good proxy for that rule on the matched cargo, tanker, and passenger ships. I reproduced every count in Section 1.4 from the repo data (4,129 matched, 3,801 of 3,873 kept, 17 false keeps, 98.1% and 99.6%). The weak points are in how the routes become RF zones, not in the routes:

- **The example band edges don't match the radars.** The widest-zone band, 3030–3070 MHz, is narrower than the 3020–3080 MHz range where IMO-category radars transmit.
- **The zone distance isn't a ship-to-receiver distance.** In shore mode, the zone distance is measured from a coastline that can be up to 25 km from the route cells.
- **The zones stop at 2900 MHz.** The study band is 2.7–3.1 GHz, and the radar front end is open across about 2700–3200 MHz.
- **The routes leave out non-SOLAS S-band users.** NTIA says these include thousands of vessels, fishing vessels among them.
- **The route product lacks EMC inputs.** It stores distinct ships per cell per day. It has no dwell time, no count of ships present at the same time, no antenna height, and no tuned frequency, so it can't feed an aggregate EMC analysis as it stands.

Several sentences also overstate what the filter shows: "best single stand-in", "keeps the ships that SOLAS requires", and "where S-band radars operate most often".

## Checked and found correct

- SOLAS V/19.2.7.1: ships of 3,000 GT and up carry a 3 GHz radar, or a second 9 GHz radar where the Administration allows it. 19.2.3.2 puts a 9 GHz radar on ships of 300 GT and up. Source: the SOLAS V URL in rf-12.
- 33 CFR 164.35(a) requires "a marine radar system for surface navigation" and names no band, as the paper says.
- IMO MSC.192(79) §5.1.2 defines S-band as 2.9–3.1 GHz and X-band as 9.2–9.5 GHz. This matches the paper's 2900–3100 MHz for 3 GHz radars.
- The U.S. table allocates 2900–3100 MHz to maritime radionavigation for both Federal and non-Federal use. Every range in Table 7 lies inside 2900–3100 MHz, the ranges don't overlap, and they cover the band from edge to edge.
- Table 7 matches `outputs/2024_2025/shipping_zone_2024_2025_band_zones.csv` and `init_shipping_zone_rev1.m:79`.
- Narrower zones nest inside wider ones: all distances are contours of one distance field (`shipping_zone_rev1.m:228-230`).
- GT proxy counts, all reproduced from repo data: 5,674 AIS ships; 4,129 matched; 3,873 of 3,000 GT or more; 3,818 kept; 3,801 true keeps; 17 false keeps; 72 misses (45 cargo, 22 passenger, 5 tanker); 98.1% recall and 99.6% precision. The median gap between AIS and registry length is 0.1 m.
- "Above about 145 m it misses more than 1 in 10": recall is 0.8947 at 145 m and 0.9166 at 140 m.
- The filter keeps ships at anchor (`min_sog_kn=0`). That's right for radar presence.
