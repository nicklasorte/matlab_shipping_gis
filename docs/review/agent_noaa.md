# NOAA reviewer: charted features, Coast Pilot, and MarineCadastre data

DRAFT//PRE-DECISIONAL

**Role.** I check the paper against what NOAA has published: ENC Direct chart services, the U.S. Coast Pilot, and the MarineCadastre AIS metadata. I don't speak for NOAA. Nothing here is a NOAA position. I queried the NOAA services on 2026-11-12; the ENC Direct services failed intermittently that day, so I retried every query until it answered. Scratch scripts and downloads are in `/tmp/agent_scratch/noaa/`.

## Summary

The island results hold up against NOAA's Marine Transportation layer. It has no lanes in the Puerto Rico or Hawaii grids, and no ENC chart scale has a racon in either grid. Every racon charted in the Alaska grid is X-band only. Five problems are bigger than that check.

1. **Lower-48 fairways.** The fairway file the lower-48 run reused holds only 28 polygons, all from a Mid-Atlantic test box. The live layer has 291 polygons in the grid. As a result, Table 3 (4,281 fairway cells, 1,099 new) misses about 8,000 charted-lane cells in the Gulf, Florida, New England, and the West Coast. One of them is the Panama City approach.
2. **Harbor-scale fairways.** The paper says NOAA charts show no fairways around Hawaii or Puerto Rico. Only the summary layer is empty. The harbor-scale ENC cells chart 54 fairway areas and 24 recommended tracks in the Hawaii grid, and 38 and 20 in the Puerto Rico grid. Several of them lead to harbors the routes miss: Kawaihae, Hilo, Mayagüez, Arecibo, Guánica, and Christiansted.
3. **Coast Pilot routes.** The Coast Pilot describes deep-draft routes that the network misses.
   - Alaska: the Shelikof Strait route from Cook Inlet to Unimak Pass. West Kodiak and the Shelikof coast of the Alaska Peninsula fall outside every zone.
   - Alaska: the port-call route north of the Aleutians.
   - Hawaii: Port Allen, Kawaihae, and the Lahaina cruise anchorage.
   - Puerto Rico: the usual route through Pasaje de San Juan and around Vieques.
4. **Mississippi.** One reach above Port Hudson on the Mississippi has no NOAA source behind it.
5. **MarineCadastre wording.** The paper's account of the data drifts from MarineCadastre's own metadata:
   - The 2021 date for dropping Alaska.
   - The reason Alaska was dropped (the Marine Exchange of Alaska's request).
   - The receiver range: the Coast Guard publishes 50 nautical miles; the paper uses 40.
   - The 2018 count drop noted in the metadata.
   - Attribute overwriting since 2024, which weakens the length cross-check.

## Checked and found correct

- **Marine Transportation layer, island grids.** It returns no features for the Puerto Rico grid (16.5–19.5° N, 64–68.5° W). The Hawaii grid (17–24° N, 153–162° W) returns only two features: the Papahānaumokuākea Particularly Sensitive Sea Area and an Area to be Avoided. The theme filter correctly leaves both out (`hawaii` and `prusvi` run logs: "Fairway cells: 0").
- **Racons, island grids.** No ENC scale (overview, general, coastal, approach, harbour) has a racon in the Puerto Rico or Hawaii grid. Layers checked: `enc_overview/10`, `enc_general/12`, `enc_coastal/14`, `enc_approach/17`, and `enc_harbour/15`. I didn't compare against the Coast Guard Light List, because the NAVCEN PDFs returned 403 or 301; that comparison is unverified.
- **Racons, Alaska grid.** The cached and live layers hold 2 (general), 16 (coastal), 6 (approach), and 1 (harbour) racons in the grid. All are charted "0.03-X" only, so "no S-band racons" is right. The only S-band racon in the grid box is at overview scale, at 69.77° N, 134.41° W. That is Canadian water east of 141° W, and the code doesn't query the overview layer anyway.
- **Racons, other scales.** The harbor-scale S-band racons (Carquinez Strait Bridge N and C; 28.83° N 89.45° W; 29.13° N 94.55° W) are all already in the general, coastal, or approach layers the code reads. No layer comes near the 1,000-record limit, so the queries didn't lose records.
- **Alaska charted lanes.** The Alaska query returns the Unimak Pass and Hinchinbrook safety fairways, the Prince William Sound and Valdez Arm schemes, and the Bering Sea and Bering Strait routing measures. These match 33 CFR 166 as printed in Coast Pilot 9 and the paper's description in Sec. 5.1.
- **Top 10 ports by ships** (`outputs/2024_2025/viz/geo_ports.csv`). The Coast Pilot 5, 2, and 7 approach channels I tested are all within 0.7 km of AIS route cells, except a few points where my own coordinates were rough. Points tested:
  - Sabine Bank Channel Lighted Buoy SB (29°25'01"N, 93°39'54"W; 0.2 km).
  - Galveston Bay Entrance, Bolivar Roads, Texas City, Bayport, Barbours Cut, and Houston turning basin.
  - Sabine Pass, Port Arthur, and Neches River.
  - Southwest Pass, Head of Passes, Plaquemines, New Orleans, and South Louisiana.
  - Angels Gate and Queens Gate.
  - Ambrose Channel, the Narrows, Kill Van Kull, Newark Bay, Arthur Kill, and Port Jersey.
- **Puerto Rico deep-draft facilities.** The facilities Coast Pilot 5 names are reached, though only four are in the port check: Bahía de Guayanilla, EcoEléctrica LNG (0.0 km); Bahía de Tallaboa (1.2 km); Laguna de Las Mareas (0.6 km); Port St. Croix (1.9 km); Mona Passage (2.7 km); Virgin Passage (0.8 km). Consider adding them to the check list.
- **Pilot boarding areas.** NOAA's `Pilot_Boarding_Areas` layer has 4 polygons in the Puerto Rico grid (St. Thomas), all within 0.9 km of route cells. In the lower 48, 22 of 25 are within 2 km of AIS cells. The 3 others are small California stations: 33.11° N 117.39° W, 34.35° N 119.88° W, and Monterey, 31 km away.
- **Alaska ports in the paper.** Valdez, Anchorage, Nikiski, and Dutch Harbor sit on Coast Pilot 9 routes. The last leg of the Strait of Juan de Fuca to Unimak Pass route and the Unimak Pass to Dutch Harbor route are on the network (median 0.3–0.4 km).
- **MarineCadastre basics.** The 2024 and 2025 records confirm that the data come from all Coast Guard NAIS terrestrial receivers, with a one-minute filter, daily CSV files compressed with Zstandard, and BOEM and NOAA OCM as publishers. The 2018 and 2019 records give an extent to 75° N, so the Alaska years the paper uses do contain Alaska.
- **No documented faults for 2024–2025.** Neither MarineCadastre record lists missing or repaired days for 2024 or 2025. The 104 sampled days range from 1,639 to 2,073 large ships (median 1,835.5), and none falls more than 15% from the median (`outputs/2024_2025/viz/viz_daily.csv`).
- **2025 column changes.** The reader finds columns by name in either style (`ais_cells_py_rev1.py:124-141`). The 2025 renames, the swapped longitude and latitude order, and the "IMO" prefix don't break the filter.
