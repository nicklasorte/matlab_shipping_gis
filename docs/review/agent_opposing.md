# Opposing-reader review: large-ship routes and frequency-dependent zones

DRAFT//PRE-DECISIONAL

Reviewer: `opposing` (AI agent). Role: a skeptical, technically strong outside party that wants the zones smaller or gone. Scope: `docs/review/paper_under_review.md`, the MATLAB and Python code, and the committed outputs. Scratch work is in `/tmp/agent_scratch/opposing/` (`ships_vs_shore.py`, `thresh.py`).

## Summary

The route data are careful and well checked, but an opposing party would not attack the routes much. It would attack how the routes become zones. The hardest objection to answer is that a "25 km zone" isn't 25 km from the ships. Shore mode treats all water within 25 km of any route cell as ship water. It then measures land distance from the coastline next to that water. On a 0.05° check grid, land in the lower-48 25 km zone sits up to 49 km from the nearest route cell. About 84,000 km², or 22% of that zone's land, is more than 25 km from any route cell. Shore mode adds about 26%, 10%, and 3% to the land area at 25, 50, and 100 km, compared with zones measured from the route cells. Next in strength: the route rule is a presence test with no link to interference; the Alaska satellite lanes rest on weak agreement between datasets; carriage of an S-band radar is assumed to mean use; and the example distances have no basis. The stability numbers in Appendix A.6 come from a different network than the paper's, and the script that made them no longer runs as committed. Changing the route threshold matters less than an opponent might hope. Requiring both years cuts the 25 km land area by about 6%, and requiring 10 or more ships cuts it by about 8%. The 25 km shore link and the zone distances drive the area.

## Checked and found correct

- Table 3 cell counts match `outputs/2024_2025/shipping_zone_2024_2025_run.log` (658,865 AIS; 15,782 port approaches; 1,099 fairway; 17 racon). The lower-48 land areas in the README match the log.
- Shore mode is a superset of ships mode, as the code comment says: `min(dist_ship, dist_shore)` with ship water set to 0 (`shipping_zone_rev1.m:214-216`).
- The Alaska satellite numbers match the outputs: cutoff 271.9, 400 hexagons, 290 beyond shore range, and 318,134 cells "not already route cells" (the JSON's 334,651 counts all hexagon water cells). The Section 5.3 sensitivity (+4/+3/+2%, −8/−7/−6%) matches `sensitivity/alaska_cutoff_sensitivity.csv`.
- The Alaska, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico port gaps in the paper match the port-check CSVs (Kivalina 11.9 km, Hilo 5.0 km, Kawaihae 24.8 km, Mayaguez 29.7 km).
- Kapsar dataset facts (625 km² hexagons, about 27 km across, satellite AIS, cargo and tanker by ship-type code) match the data paper ([PMC9436749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436749/)).
- The SOLAS V/19.2.7.1 wording in Section 1.1 is accurate ([UK MCA SOLAS V](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f0081ed915d74e33f3c6e/solas_v_on_safety_of_navigation.pdf)).
- `period_rev1.m` and `python/ship_period.py` both use the first 52 Wednesdays of 2024 and 2025, as Section 1.3 says.
- Table 7 matches `shipping_zone_2024_2025_band_zones.csv`.
- NOAA's 2024 AIS Vessel Transit Counts raster in `data/ref/` stops at 51.7° N, which supports the paper's statement that no 2024 AIS covers Alaska in that product.
- The lower-48 fairway cache holds 28 features and includes no "Area to be Avoided" polygons in the kept theme layers.
