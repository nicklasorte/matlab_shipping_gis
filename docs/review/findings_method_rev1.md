The findings that matter most:

- **The lower-48 fairway cache is truncated (CA-1, CA-2).** It holds 28 features from three Mid-Atlantic approaches. The live layer holds 291. A name mismatch also drops every "Traffic Separation Schemes" polygon. With the live layer, my rasterization adds 8,794 charted-lane cells that aren't AIS cells, 3,627 of them in the Gulf. Adding the dropped schemes makes it 11,893. Table 3 reports 1,099.
- **The water-and-land zone files count foreign land as ship water (CA-3).** Vancouver, Victoria, Toronto, Tijuana, Havana, and Bimini all fall inside the 25 km band file.
- **The zone distance isn't a distance from the ships (CA-4 to CA-7).** In Hawaii, land in the 25 km zone sits up to 49.6 km from a route cell. Where the coast is more than 25 km from the routes, the rule works the other way. Newport, Oregon, and Kalaloch, Washington, fall outside the 25 km land zone.
- **Two-ship cells make the Mississippi route above Baton Rouge (CA-8).** The 4-ship cells end at 30.485° N. Sections 1.7 and A.4 contradict each other.
- **The reported land areas are raster counts (CA-33).** They run about 5% to 8% above the shipped land polygons on the islands.

Some sub-claims didn't hold. They appear as notes in the Partly confirmed rows. For example, the EEZ clip that `uscg-5` describes doesn't show in the data: 97,350 Pacific route cells lie off Mexico.
